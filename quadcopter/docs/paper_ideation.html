ABSTRACT
We present an autonomous, end-to-end framework for generative mechanical design that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) as cognitive optimization engines. Unlike traditional gradient-based topology optimization or parametric sweep algorithms, our system establishes a continuous loop of geometry generation, multi-physics validation (finite element analysis and computational fluid dynamics), metric evaluation, and model update. By deploying local reasoning models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Coder and Phi-4) on consumer-grade hardware (NVIDIA GTX 1650), we demonstrate that LLMs can interpret raw simulation logs, understand structural/aerodynamic failure modes, and iteratively mutate 3D CAD parameters (implemented via FreeCAD) to optimize custom parts. The framework is validated through the design and optimization of a high-performance quadcopter propeller, demonstrating significant improvements in lift-to-drag ratios while maintaining structural constraints under centrifugal and aerodynamic loads.
Keywords: Autonomous Agents, Generative Engineering, Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), FreeCAD, OpenFOAM, Large Language Models (LLMs).

1. Introduction

The field of computer-aided engineering (CAE) has traditionally relied on human designers to bridge the gap between design generation (CAD) and physics validation (CFD/FEA). When a simulation indicates that a component fails to meet structural or aerodynamic criteria, a human engineer interprets the physical field data, identifies the root causes, and manually edits CAD parameters to mitigate the issues. While automated optimization methods such as Topology Optimization (TO) and parametric sweep algorithms exist, they are computationally intensive, struggle with multi-disciplinary objectives, and require exact gradient formulations or highly restricted parameter bounds.

In parallel, Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown significant promise in reasoning over complex, structured text, including code and configuration files. Recently, frameworks such as Foam-Agent 2.0 have utilized LLMs for automated simulation configuration and diagnostic repair. However, these applications have remained confined to setup and troubleshooting within the simulation domain, leaving the actual geometry modification to human operators or external heuristics.

This paper introduces a closed-loop, LLM-in-the-Loop Generative Mechanical Design framework. We delegate the entire cycle of CAD modification, mesh generation, physics simulation, result extraction, and strategic parameter adjustments to a unified multi-agent system. The core contribution is demonstrating that an LLM can act as a heuristic director of physical optimization, interpreting raw numeric data from FEA and CFD solvers to make rational, goal-oriented changes to 3D geometries.

2. System Architecture

The proposed framework consists of four primary phases executing in a continuous loop:

2.1 CAD Generator (FreeCAD/Python API)

A parametric Python script utilizes the FreeCAD API to generate 3D solid geometry (specifically STL and STEP representations). The geometry is parameterized by variables (e.g., blade thickness, chord distribution, tubercle depth, and count) that the LLM is authorized to mutate.

2.2 Physics Solver Stack (CalculiX & OpenFOAM)

The generated STL files are dispatched to a multi-physics evaluation pipeline:

CFD (OpenFOAM): A Dockerized OpenFOAM setup scales the geometry, builds an unstructured hex-dominant mesh using snappyHexMesh, and runs a steady-state aerodynamic solver (simpleFoam) with k-omega SST turbulence modeling.

FEA (CalculiX): The geometry undergoes structural analysis to calculate maximum Von Mises stress and displacement under design thrust and rotational velocity.

2.3 Evaluator & LLM Reasoning Engine

The simulation output logs, including lift, drag, and stress distributions, are compiled into a compact performance report. A local LLM (running via Ollama) processes this report alongside the active parameter set. Leveraging its reasoning capabilities, the LLM analyzes the trade-offs (e.g., thinning a blade to reduce drag versus thickening it to withstand aerodynamic bending stress) and outputs a proposed set of CAD parameters for the next iteration.

3. Preliminary Results & Discussion

Initial results from the baseline CFD case (five-blade propeller with tubercle design) demonstrate stable convergence behavior in the simpleFoam solver. The pressure and viscous force components are logged at regular intervals, confirming steady thrust generation of approximately 18.15 N. Crucially, the scaling pipeline successfully avoids STL file degradation by applying transformations on fresh geometry templates rather than cumulative transformations.

4. Conclusion & Future Work

This paper demonstrates that closed-loop generative engineering using LLMs is feasible on consumer-grade hardware. By automating the CAD-to-CFD-to-CAD pipeline, we establish a prototype for autonomous physical optimization. Future work will investigate multi-agent architectures where separate agents specialize in meshing diagnostics, structural validation, and CAD synthesis, respectively.
